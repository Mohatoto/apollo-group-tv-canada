---
title: Wi-Fi or Ethernet for Streaming: Which Is Better?
description: When a network cable beats Wi-Fi for streaming, when Wi-Fi is fine, and how to get a steady picture with either one.
---
An Ethernet cable gives the steadiest picture, because it is not affected by distance, walls or other networks. Wi-Fi is fine for most streaming when the device is close to the router. Use a cable for your main TV if you can, and Wi-Fi for everything that moves.

## Why a cable is steadier

Wi-Fi is a radio signal. It gets weaker with distance and with every wall and floor it passes through, and it shares the air with your neighbours' networks, microwaves and cordless phones. A cable carries the full speed from the router to the device, with almost no variation.

For streaming, steadiness matters more than top speed. A connection that briefly drops from 50 Mbps to 2 Mbps causes a pause, even though the average looks fine.

## When Wi-Fi is good enough

- The device is in the same room as the router, or the next room.
- A speed test on the device shows at least the speed you need: 10 Mbps or more for HD and 25 Mbps or more for 4K, as listed by Apollo Group TV Canada.
- You rarely see pauses in the evening, when networks are busiest.

## When to use a cable

- The TV is far from the router and the picture pauses or drops in quality.
- You watch a lot of live TV, which cannot load far ahead.
- You watch in 4K.
- Several people stream at once and the main TV is taking most of the Wi-Fi.

Many smart TVs and streaming boxes have an Ethernet port. Some streaming sticks need a small adapter, sold by the device maker.

## Getting more from Wi-Fi

1. **Place the router well.** Central, raised, in the open, and not inside a cabinet.
2. **Choose the right band.** The 5 GHz network is faster at short range. The 2.4 GHz network reaches further but is slower and more crowded.
3. **Restart the router now and then.** It clears faults that build up over weeks.
4. **Consider a mesh system** for a large home. It places extra access points so that far rooms get a strong signal.

For the channel, channel width and QoS settings inside the router, see [the best router settings for streaming](/guides/router-settings-for-streaming/).

## If you cannot run a cable

Powerline adapters send the network through your home's electrical wiring, and a mesh access point placed near the TV can provide an Ethernet port. Either one is usually steadier than a weak direct Wi-Fi signal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Ethernet faster than Wi-Fi?

Often, but the main gain is consistency. A cable delivers nearly the same speed all the time, which is what prevents pauses.

### Does the type of Ethernet cable matter?

For streaming, any cable marked Cat 5e or higher is more than enough.

### Will a Wi-Fi extender fix buffering?

It can help in a room with a weak signal, but basic extenders also cut speed. A mesh system or a cable is a more reliable fix.
